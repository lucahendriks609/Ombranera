a purchase-oriented goal, product presented as a sellable object. The live site is deliberately

## Original brief, verbatim

> OMBRA NERA — Landing Page Wireframe Brief
>
> This is not a conversion-optimization brief. Do not apply SaaS/growth-marketing patterns: no
> urgency mechanics, no objection-handling framing, no trust-badge social proof, no benefit-selling
> feature tiles. The brand states, it does not sell. Every section below is built on that
> constraint, not around it.
>
> **Context**
> - Product: DOMINIO / VÉSPERA / HERITAGE
> - Target audience: men who already reject mainstream niche fragrance; old money-adjacent, quiet
>   luxury sensibility; low volume, high conviction — not a broad demo
> - Primary goal: Inner Circle access request / direct Extrait sale — no funnel language, no
>   lead-gen framing
> - Tone: cold, precise, restrained on the surface; the darkness lives in the Confessions, not the
>   layout
> - Brand system: matte black, spaced silver typography, smoked glass — fixed, not a design choice
>   per project
>
> **Task**
>
> Design a wireframe brief for a landing page. For each section, top to bottom:
>
> 1. **Hero** — No headline formula. A single declarative statement — can be a Confession fragment.
>    Subline, if present, is functional only (fragrance name, concentration — not a value prop). CTA:
>    one word, quiet, non-imperative ("Enter," not "Shop Now"). No arrows, no pulsing buttons, no
>    urgency copy. Hero image: cold and restrained — product or atmosphere, never aspirational
>    lifestyle stock.
>
> 2. **Proof section** (not a "social proof bar") — No logo rows, no star ratings, no trust badges —
>    borrowed credibility on display contradicts the brand's authority. If used at all: one curated
>    reviewer citation, sourced, placed editorially. Before reviewer relationships exist, this section
>    does not appear. Absence is acceptable.
>
> 3. **World / Confession section** (not "problem/solution") — The brand doesn't diagnose a pain
>    point and resolve it. This section carries a raw Confession excerpt — no framing, no
>    introduction, no "here's what's wrong, here's our fix" structure. Text stands alone.
>
> 4. **Fragrance architecture** (not "feature showcase") — Note pyramid and character presented as
>    fact, not benefit. No "why choose us" tiles. Single column or restrained grid — structure
>    follows the note pyramid's own hierarchy (top/heart/base), not a marketing layout convention.
>
> 5. **Citation section** (not "testimonial carousel") — No carousel, no star ratings, no reviewer
>    headshots. If included: one or two plain-text citations, sourced by name. Static, not animated.
>    Credibility comes from who said it, not how many said it.
>
> 6. **Price section** (not "pricing/CTA with urgency mechanics") — Price stated once, plainly. No
>    countdowns, no "only X left," no discount framing, no bundling pressure. If Discovery Set exists
>    as entry point, it sits here as a parallel option, not an upsell trick.
>
> 7. **Information section** (not "FAQ — objection handling") — No persuasion frame — the brand
>    doesn't argue for itself. Plain factual answers only: concentration, ingredients, shipping,
>    returns. No question is phrased to overcome resistance ("But what if it doesn't work for me?").
>    Phrase as neutral fact-finding.
>
> For each section, describe: layout (left/right/centered), approximate height, content hierarchy,
> and one design principle — drawn from restraint and negative space (e.g. "silence as premium
> signal," "absence over embellishment"), not standard conversion-UX heuristics.
>
> Standing rule reminder for whoever executes this brief: if a request during build (collab
> placement, discount module, countdown timer, comparison table) conflicts with the above, the
> answer is no — state that explicitly rather than softening the section around it.
