Fragrances don’t lie. Everything else can be controlled — what you say, how you dress, how you enter a room. But what you wear tells the truth about the parts of you that you never put into words. The Confessions exist for that. Not to describe the scent. To describe the person wearing it — the version of him that doesn’t introduce itself.

Dominio

I have never had to introduce myself.

Not out of arrogance. Simply because the room already knows before I say anything. There is a moment, always the same moment, when people decide whether to fear someone or not. That moment has already passed by the time they realise it.

I handle things. That is all you need to know. I decide who prospers and who disappears. Who sits at the table and who stands outside in the rain. Not because I want to — because that is simply how it is. The world needs a structure. I am that structure.

They asked me once if I had ever been afraid.

I had to think about it. Not because the answer was difficult. Because the question struck me as so strange — like asking whether water has ever been dry.

There is smoke and resin and something too old to have a name. Something that smouldered long before I arrived and will keep smouldering long after everything you know has ceased to exist. Power does not announce itself. It simply remains.

— Doubt ends where control begins.

Véspera

I never move toward anything.

Not because I don’t want. Because I understood a long time ago that desire that shows itself is the first thing to disappear. So I stayed still. Learned to be something warm underneath — amber and suede and the kind of vanilla you only notice after the person has already left the room.

They always come.

That is not a trick. I do nothing. There is simply something about a person who does not need — no confirmation, no attention, no gesture in their direction. It is the quietest kind of power that exists and the only one I have ever believed in.

A woman told me once that I felt like a decision already made.

I didn’t know if she meant it as a compliment. It didn’t matter to me.

I watch. I wait. I stay in the room without moving while the room slowly shifts toward me, the way it always does. Sandalwood and honey and something sweet you cannot point to but will never forget.

I have never chased anything. Everything that was mine has come.

— Silence is the strongest invitation there is.

Heritage

I have outlasted all of them.

My brother. My wife. The people I loved before I understood what it costs to love someone in a world like this. They are gone — not from age, not from illness. From men. Specific men with specific reasons that made sense to them and will never make sense to me.

I am the youngest of my family. I am the only one still standing.

The cognac is good. The silence is not. But I have learned the difference between loneliness and solitude — and I choose to call this solitude. It helps sometimes.

There are things beneath my bed I hope I never need again.

I have lived a life that was serious — cigars and coffee and the smoke of a man who performs the same ritual every evening because continuity is the only thing he has left. Sandalwood and amber and bourbon, the luxury of a man who decided long ago how things end and is no longer in any hurry.

I am not bitter. I am old and I am sharp and I am very, very patient. Dominio took them. Serpente took them. And I sit here — the only one still standing — with good whiskey and a long memory and all the time in the world.

They should know I am still here.

— Old. Sharp. Still here.
